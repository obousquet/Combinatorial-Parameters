Projected Distinguishing Range / Proper Ordered Sample Compression

Name: Projected Distinguishing Range / Proper Ordered Sample Compression

Parameter 1: Projected Distinguishing Range

Parameter 2: Proper Ordered Sample Compression

Relationship Type: $P_1\ge P_2$

Variant:

Details:

For every finite binary class, $\mathrm R^p(\mathcal H)\geq\mathrm{pOSC}(\mathcal H)$.

Witness: Half-intervals

Witness Strength: Unbounded gap

Witness Verification:

For half-intervals on $[n]$, $\mathrm R^p=n-1$, whereas the proper ordered sample-compression value is one.

Status: Established

Proof or Proof Sketch:

Fix a proper ordered scheme and consider any inclusion-minimal teaching sample used by its compression rule, for a hypothesis $h$ relative to the later hypotheses in the defining order. Write $T$ for its coordinate set. For each $x\in T$, minimality supplies a later hypothesis $h_x$ agreeing with $h$ on $T\setminus\{x\}$ and differing at $x$. Thus the projection of the relevant tail class to $T$ contains the star consisting of $h_{|T}$ and the $|T|$ distinct one-coordinate flips $(h_x)_{|T}$. Every coordinate of this star is needed to distinguish its centre from its corresponding leaf, so its distinguishing range is $|T|$. The full projected class has distinguishing range at least that of this subfamily, hence $\mathrm R^p(\mathcal H)\geq|T|$. This holds for every compressed minimal teaching sample, so it bounds the scheme size; minimizing over proper ordered schemes gives $\mathrm R^p\geq\mathrm{pOSC}$.
