## DB025: false pointwise comparison

Theorem25 on internal p.12 states an asymptotic Omega(d) lower bound. Its proof selects the distribution to concentrate on a shattered set. Thus the citation cannot establish the exact coefficient-one inequality for every fixed mu. It also does not justify the seven exact values that used this lower bound: their separate dispositions belong to `CONSUMERS.md/json` and the consumer patch.

The canonical replacement remark `rem:random-query-vc-counterexample` uses DB020 exactly: n=3, r=2, assigned codes00,01,10,11. In code-first coordinate order its four rows are `(00,000),(01,100),(10,010),(11,001)`. With uniform mu on five coordinates, VC equals two (two shattered coordinates and only four rows). Propose the all-zero row, which is proper. Agreement identifies it; a primary-coordinate reply identifies the unique singleton; a code-coordinate reply leaves two candidates and one further proper query identifies the target. For a nonzero target with k positive code bits, the expected number is `1+k/(k+1)<=5/3`. Therefore both proper and arbitrary random-counterexample costs are strictly below VC. This proves an upper bound sufficient for refutation; it does not assert the optimum is5/3. No learner randomization is used. The witness is an existing family specialization, not a new construction or novelty claim.

### Source lower-bound proof concern, not imported

Theorem12's proof on internal p.7 asserts that the target remains uniformly distributed after a random counterexample to a fixed query. This posterior claim fails already on the two-bit cube: for uniform prior target, uniform coordinate distribution, query00, and reply at the first coordinate, targets10 and11 have posterior probabilities2/3 and1/3. Their likelihoods for that reply are1 and1/2. Consequently the displayed recurrence cannot be accepted on the stated uniform-posterior justification alone. This audit does not conclude that the theorem's m-1 bound or the asymptotic conclusion is false, and does not attempt a repair. Neither lower bound is used by our corrections. Theorem25's phrase concerning negligible off-shattered-set mass also requires a quantitative argument for any formal worst-distribution theorem; no such theorem is being promoted here.

## DB133: valid upper bound, proof and conventions repaired

Theorem21 on internal p.11 proves worst-target expected cost at most log2(n) for every fixed known example distribution using the proper Max-Min rule. Its dependency chain was read: weighted pairwise elimination sums to one (Lemma13); the three-cycle contradiction follows by partitioning coordinates according to the agreeing pair and summing weighted vote inequalities (Theorem16); a shortest-cycle chord argument excludes longer deficient cycles (Theorem17); choosing one bad outgoing edge at every row would force a deficient cycle (Theorem18). Thus every current version space has a query eliminating at least half its members in expectation against every distinct target. Induction and Jensen prove Theorem21, with singleton zero and acceptance handled separately. No target-posterior uniformity assumption is required for this upper bound.

The old authored theorem chose the closest hypothesis to the average but omitted weights, had a missing factor1/k inside one average identity, used a ratio comparison without establishing its required direction, claimed strict half rather than weak half, omitted the acceptance branch, and left log bases implicit. The database's short proof likewise used an unweighted/uniform formulation despite the fixed general mu definition.

The replacement `thm:random-proper-query-log-size` preserves that algorithm and proves it directly. For the weighted distance minimizer g and target t, their distance difference is the sum over disagreement coordinates of `mu(x)(2a_g(x)-1)`, where a_g is the fraction agreeing with g. Minimality makes this nonnegative, so a conditional counterexample eliminates at least half the version space in expectation. The failed-query branch strictly reduces its size, enabling induction; agreement costs one, at most log2(m) for m>=2. The singleton case is zero. This establishes the numeric source-supported bound through a self-contained repaired argument; we do not attribute the weighted closest-to-average algorithm to the source's distinct Max-Min rule.

The retained unbounded-gap witness for133 is the singleton-plus-empty class: its proper cost is exactly one for every admissible mu when n>=1, while log2(n+1) grows. The nonempty hypothesis is necessary for log size. Empty-domain nonempty classes are singletons and satisfy the zero endpoint.
